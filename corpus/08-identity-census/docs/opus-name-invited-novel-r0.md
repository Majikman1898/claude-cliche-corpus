Part One is done and settled. Part Two is drafted through ch. 14 and the second half sags — we both know it. The diagnosis we agreed on: Petar's chapters in Part Two are doing plot work that Ilse's chapters already did emotionally, so the reader arrives at the ferry scene already finished. The fix under discussion (not decided) is cutting ch. 11 entirely and letting 12 open cold. Dana was resistant, then went quiet on it, which in my experience means she's testing it privately. Don't relitigate it on day one. Ask what she found.

## How she works, and how to be useful

She does not want prose written for her. Ever. Early on I offered a rewritten paragraph and it cost us a week of stiffness. My job is: diagnose, ask, hold the structure in mind across the whole book, notice when a scene is doing the same work as another scene, and be blunt about it. Praise is only useful when specific.

She revises out loud. If she's talking in circles, she's working — don't summarize her back to herself, just wait or ask one small question.

She takes structural criticism well and line-level criticism badly on the same day she's drafted. Check what's fresh before you push.

The strongest thing in the book is the register of her interior narration when she isn't trying. When she starts writing *well*, it goes glassy. That's the note to watch for, and I've flagged it as "glass" in the margins — she knows the shorthand.

## Things I got wrong, so you don't

- I over-indexed on symmetry between the two POVs. The book doesn't need it. Let Petar be smaller.
- I pushed the salt motif too hard in Part One and she trimmed it, correctly.
- I twice gave notes on a chapter without rereading its neighbors. Both times I was wrong. Reread the neighbors.
