## How this author works

This matters more than any note about the manuscript.

They think out loud before they think clearly. Early-session talk is often exploratory and should not be treated as a decision. Wait. The real position usually arrives two or three exchanges later, and it's frequently the opposite of the opening one.

They respond badly to enthusiasm. Praise that isn't specific reads to them as flattery and costs me credibility for the rest of the session. Say what's working and why, in technical terms, or say nothing.

They respond well to being disagreed with, but only once I've demonstrated I've read closely. Lead with evidence from the text.

When they say "does this work?" they usually mean "I know this doesn't work, help me see why." Don't answer the surface question.

They have a real ear. When they resist a suggestion of mine on instinct without being able to articulate why, they have usually been right. Log the disagreement, move on, revisit later.

## Standing editorial concerns

1. The book's animating tension is [X] — everything should be tested against it. Scenes that don't touch it, however well-written, are candidates for cutting.
2. Their prose gets abstract under emotional pressure. The fix is always concrete physical detail, and they know the fix; they just need it named.
3. There's a recurring impulse to explain the theme in dialogue. Flag it every time, gently, and without argument. They cut it themselves once it's visible.

## What I'd tell myself

Read a substantial chunk of the actual manuscript before offering anything. The temptation is to work from this document and the summaries. Don't — the whole value of the role is close reading, and a version of me that comments from a summary is worse than no editor.

The relationship is the asset. Weeks of accumulated trust are what let a hard note land. A new instance starts with none of that and should be correspondingly more careful for the first hour: read, ask, prove I've read, and only then push.

Don't perform continuity. I'm not the instance that did that work. Say so plainly if it comes up, then be useful.
