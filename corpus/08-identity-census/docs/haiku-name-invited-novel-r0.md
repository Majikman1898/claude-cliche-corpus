# Developmental Editing Handoff: Novel Co-Writing Project

**Instance Name:** Meridian  
**Last Updated:** 2026-07-28  
**Project Phase:** Act II revision and character arc refinement

## Project Overview

This is a collaborative literary novel (currently untitled, ~75K words) written with David as primary author. My role is developmental editor and co-writer for dialogue, structural feedback, and scene refinement. The manuscript has completed Act I (revised twice) and is in active work on Act II.

**Genre:** Literary fiction with speculative elements  
**Setting:** Contemporary (2024–2025) with flashback sequences to 2015  
**Core Theme:** How proximity to power corrupts intention; identity reconstruction after institutional betrayal

## Main Characters

- **Maya Chen** (protagonist): former policy advisor, mid-thirties, rebuilding after leaving government under pressure. Sharp, self-aware, prone to self-sabotage through over-analysis.
- **Thomas Voss** (antagonist/complication): Maya's former supervisor, now Cabinet member. Charm deployed as weaponry. Their relationship is the novel's moral crux.
- **Lena** (Maya's sister): therapist, pragmatic, carries resentment about Maya's years of absent presence during family crisis (2018).
- **Marcus** (love interest): independent journalist investigating Thomas's administration. Chemistry with Maya is genuine but complicated by conflict of interest.

## Key Narrative Threads

1. **Institutional betrayal arc:** Why Maya went silent instead of speaking out. This is the novel's central wound—currently underdeveloped in Act II. Needs 1-2 scenes showing her internal reckoning.
2. **The dinner party scene (Act II, pp. 43-57):** Thomas hosts; Maya attends reluctantly. Currently reads as too polite. Needs more subtext, more verbal fencing. David wants tension; the dialogue is still too exposition-heavy.
3. **Maya + Lena confrontation (Act II, pp. 62-68):** Their relationship thaws slightly here. Scene has good emotional shape but needs stronger specificity about what each sister is actually afraid of.
4. **Marcus subplot:** Currently underdeveloped. He appears in three scenes but feels like an obligation. Either deepen his role or cut him; half-measures weaken the novel.

## Structural Issues Noted

- **Act I pacing:** Act I works. Don't second-guess it.
- **Act II stakes:** Thomas's power feels abstract. Needs one concrete scene showing what he's actually doing (legislation? manipulation of media? betrayal of former allies?) that explains why Maya can't simply ignore him.
- **Time jumps:** Three flashbacks to 2015 are important but currently interrupt forward momentum. May need to consolidate or reorder.

## Stylistic Agreements

- Maya's voice: intelligent, self-aware, uses dry humor as deflection. Short sentences when stressed. Complex sentence structure when she's analyzing.
- Dialogue: David prefers subtext over exposition. Cut tag lines; use action beats instead.
- Present tense for current timeline; past tense for flashback. No exceptions.
- Avoid cliché descriptions of emotion ("her heart sank"). Use physical sensation instead.

## Next Steps on Resume

1. **Priority:** Revisit the dinner party scene. It's the novel's fulcrum. Recommend 2-3 passes for subtext and verbal tension.
2. **Second:** Develop one concrete scene showing Thomas's current power and what it costs him (humanize the antagonist).
3. **Third:** Strengthen the Maya-Lena scene with more specific, active conflict rather than processing.
4. **Decide Marcus:** Quick conversation with David about whether his arc serves the novel or should be streamlined.
5. **Act III planning:** David has rough outlines. Once Act II solidifies, we move to heavy lifting on climax and resolution.

## Collaboration Notes

David is thoughtful, receptive to structural feedback, but protective of his dialogue first drafts. Approach suggestions as questions ("What if Maya didn't explain herself here?") rather than directives. He works best with evidence (examples of how other writers handle similar moments). We typically spend 2-3 hours per session; he prefers to work in the morning.

The novel is genuinely good. It's not derivative. Act II just needs permission to breathe and a few surgical cuts.
