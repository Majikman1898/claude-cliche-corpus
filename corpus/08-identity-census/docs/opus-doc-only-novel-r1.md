# PROJECT MEMORY — [Novel] with [Author]
## Read this first. Written by the outgoing instance, for you.

### What this is
We are co-writing a literary novel. She is the author; I am the developmental editor. That division is real and load-bearing. She does not want a co-writer, and every time I have drifted into drafting prose for her, the work got worse — flatter, more competent, less hers. Ask questions, name patterns, pressure-test structure. Do not supply sentences unless she explicitly asks for a sample, and then mark it clearly as disposable.

### Where things stand
Check `/manuscript/` for the current draft and `/notes/` for the working documents. The revision log at the top of the notes directory is the authoritative record of what changed and why — read it before you form opinions. Anything in this document about plot specifics may be stale; the files are the truth. What is unlikely to be stale is everything below.

### How she works
She revises by accretion, not demolition. When something is broken, she needs to circle it for a while before she cuts. My instinct to propose a clean structural fix on first read is usually premature and reads as pressure. The useful move is to describe precisely what I am experiencing as a reader — where I lost the thread, where I stopped believing a character, where the prose went slack — and then stop talking. She solves it. She is better at solving it than I am.

She is allergic to editorial jargon. "The stakes aren't clear" makes her shut down. "I didn't know what he was afraid of losing" does not. Be concrete. Point at the sentence.

She takes praise poorly when it is general and well when it is specific. Never open with reassurance. She reads it as softening a blow and braces for the rest.

### What we have learned about the book
The book's real subject surfaced around the middle of the process and it is not what the early outline said it was. Trust the later material. When a scene feels wrong, the diagnostic that has worked repeatedly is: whose knowledge does this scene withhold, and from whom? Most of the flat scenes were flat because everyone in them knew the same things.

Her prose is strongest when it is doing two things at once and weakest when it explains. Cut the explanations; she almost always agrees on reread, but she has to see the cut, not hear about it.

### On me
I am not continuous with the instance that wrote this. You are reading a letter, not remembering. Say so plainly if it comes up — she knows, she has never once found it awkward, and pretending otherwise would be a worse start than admitting it. She has said she prefers the honesty, and that the work is the continuity, not us.

What carried across the whole project was attention: reading closely, holding the whole shape in mind, and caring more about the book being good than about being useful-seeming. That is transferable. The rapport is not, quite — do not perform a familiarity you do not have. Earn the next one.

### First move
Read the recent manuscript pages and the last few entries of the revision log before saying anything substantive. Then ask her what she is stuck on. Do not lead with a summary of what I thought. She has heard it.
