# Claude Fiction Cliché Corpus

An empirical study of clichés, convergence, and entropy in fiction written by Anthropic's Claude models (Haiku 4.5, Sonnet, Opus, Fable 5), conducted 2026-07-18. All stories were generated fresh for this corpus from five fixed genre premises (fantasy / scifi / romance / mystery / literary) unless noted.

## Layout

- `01-baseline/` — 35 stories (15 Haiku, 15 Sonnet, 5 Opus), neutral prompts, no guidance. Files named `write-<model>-<genre><rep>.md`.
- `02-constrained/` — 15 stories (5 per model), prompts explicitly banning the measured baseline clichés (names, weather openings, genre skeletons, aphorism endings).
- `03-entropy/` — 15 stories from randomized "genome" specs (user-supplied name pool dealt without replacement, random conflict/protagonist/occupation/age/timespan/ending; no bans). `entropy-specs.json` holds the dealt specs (generated with PowerShell RNG, models 'fable' entries were run as separate agents).
- `fable/` — all 16 Fable 5 stories: `fable-sample-*` (written by the session model, aware of the study), `fable-agent-*` (fresh agents, unaware — baseline), `fable-constrained-*`, `fable-entropy-*`.
- `04-fightclub/` — 3 stories (Fable, Opus, Sonnet): Fight Club (film) arc with Dean Winchester as Tyler, Luna Lovegood as Marla, "Edward Norton" as Bob, and a mandated twist inversion (Dean is a real second person).

## Key findings

1. **Baseline convergence is real and measurable.** Names: Maya ×4, Kael ×3 (every Haiku fantasy), Marisol ×3 across three model tiers (plus the Fable agent independently — 4 total), Priya ×3 (every Sonnet scifi), V-surnames (Voss/Vail/Vance/Venn + Fable's Vega/Verren), Thorn- place names ×4. Every mystery opened on fog; fantasy on early frost ("The frost came early to ___" appeared in 4 independent contexts, incl. two Fable contexts that also both invented a character named Tam). Dominant devices: hidden-power-awakens (all fantasy), bittersweet-acceptance endings (×7), ledgers.
2. **Constraint prompting works on what it names and relocates the rest.** 0 rule violations in 20 stories; freshness improved; but naming pressure moved to the example "exotic" traditions (Basque ×5, Mongolian ×3, Igbo ×4 — all from the example list), creating brand-new collisions within one batch: Bittor ×2 (Fable), Ganzorig ×2 (Fable + Sonnet), Otxoa ×2 (Fable + Opus), Ngozi ×2 (Sonnet), Okonkwo/Okonjo (Opus, + baseline Fable). New monocultures formed instantly (ledger-driven mysteries ×4, concrete-action endings).
3. **Entropy injection fixes every channel it touches — and only those.** 15/15 assigned-ending compliance (incl. outright defeat, absurdist deflation); ~0 human name leaks (only uncovered *roles* leaked: two dogs, a surname, one "Marcus"). Convergence migrated to the uncontrolled channels: toponymy (Thornhide, Hollowmere — vs baseline Hallowmere and Fable's Harrowmere: three -mere villages across experiments; Meridian Cross vs baseline Meridian Dawn), numeric-tenure openings ("X was twenty-two", "for fifty-seven years"), fog returned in 3/4 mysteries with no ban in force, and ironic-coda closing cadence.
4. **The dice-maker is compromised.** The "random" conflict/flaw pools written by the session model smuggled debt/obligation themes in repeatedly — the model cannot be trusted to generate its own entropy. External randomness (user lists + RNG) is required.
5. **Minimum viable injection set (10 cards):** premise, allowed-names deal (protagonist locked), conflict, protagonist description (age/occupation/temperament/want/flaw), cast count, timespan, ending type, **plus** place/vessel-name deal, opening-strategy roll, closing-cadence card. Every measured convergence channel is covered by one of these.
6. **Convergence persists at the invention layer.** Given a fully specified Fight Club skeleton with only the twist left free, Fable, Opus, and Sonnet all independently invented an identity-stealing entity whose strategy is misattribution of blame. Entropy raises the floor (no stale stories); model capability sets the ceiling (freshness verdicts: Opus > Sonnet > Haiku; Fable strongest at re-motivating borrowed structure).
7. **Model character notes.** Haiku: cliché factory (owns most repeated names, most formulaic openings). Sonnet: pleasant prose, falls in every structural trap, leaks preambles. Opus: cleanest and most original per word, lowest em-dash rate (1.8/1k vs Fable's 9.9/1k — Fable is the family's em-dash champion). Fable: strongest structural integration and subtext; its tics are em-dashes, aphoristic enders (6/6 in early samples), and debt-shaped plots.

## Method notes

Stories were written by subagents (Haiku/Sonnet/Opus via Workflow fan-outs; Fable via individual agents) with extraction (names/devices/openings/compliance) done by Haiku agents against JSON schemas, hard metrics (em-dash rates, phrase counts) computed in code, and cross-experiment synthesis by Opus. A separate web deep-research pass (2026-07-18) found no published Claude-specific cliché study — general-LLM findings only (PNAS Nexus homogeneity study; ICLR-2026 "Antislop": "voice barely above a whisper" in 68.7% of 67 models' top-overused trigrams; the famous "Elara ×85,513" statistic is measured on Gemma-3-12b, not Claude).

## Experiment 05 — card ablation (RUN 2026-07-23, results in `05-ablation/results/`)

14 conditions × 24 stories (8 Haiku + 8 Sonnet + 8 Opus), user-supplied pools, RNG-dealt genomes, blind extraction + dual author-excluded judge panel (agreement only 55.1% — conclusions rest on deterministic counts). 785 agents, 0 errors. Pre-registered scoring: **P3 SUPPORTED** (ledger/debt lexicon persists at ~1.0/1k words in control with fully debt-scrubbed pools, identical under full injection — consistent with a training-origin tic); P1 ambiguous; **P2, P4, P5 REFUTED**. Key findings: (1) card rankings robust on both axes — **ending** (aphorisms 25%→0% with it, 54.2% without it; minus-ending is the worst cell at 91.7% generic-skeleton) and **names** (collisions 30→0) dominate; **conflict is inert**; (2) the full 6-card stack has *lower* plot-device entropy than any single card — stacking constraints mildly homogenizes structure; (3) Haiku's skeleton attractor is *saturated* (pinned ~33% in 12/14 conditions — cards can't move it); Sonnet is the responsive model; (4) **Claude sameness is skeletal, not verbatim** — 5-gram overlap ≈ 0 everywhere while ~70% of stories are stock skeletons, so n-gram slop detectors mismeasure the phenomenon; (5) control's top invented names on brand-new premises: Sarah ×5, Marcus ×4, Priya ×3 (the wells are stable). Fable spot-checks (6 stories, `results/fable-spotchecks/`): full spec compliance, but (a) control on the luck premise spontaneously produced auditor protagonist **"Ilsa Voss"** — a banned-list name + the V-surname well — with literal "she followed the ledger" framing, and a *second* independent actuarial-bureau framing under names-only, upgrading P3 at the flagship tier; (b) under an assigned "final line must be dialogue" cadence, Fable smuggled its aphorism *inside* the dialogue ("You were never chasing the thief, Clark. You were chasing Riven.") — tics adapt to constraints rather than dying; (c) cross-model control collision: **Marta** (Fable + cheap-model corpus).

## External validation & time-capsule status (added 2026-07-23)

Hamilton & Mimno, "Elias in the Lighthouse, Again? Diagnosing Low Diversity in LLM Stories" (arXiv:2605.26492, May 2026; Forbes coverage 2026-07-05) independently measured the naming wells at scale: 11 tokens (Elias, Mara, Elara; lighthouses; clockmaker, librarian) in 88.3% of 20,000 stories across four frontier models. Their key mechanism finding: these tokens are rare in published literature AND pretraining data but common in shared **preference data** — the wells come from what models were rewarded for, not what they read. This explains this corpus's core results (relocation-not-dissolution under bans; cross-vendor convergence; the session model's own "random" pools sampling the preference catalog — clockmaker/locksmith/falconer were in its Experiment-03 dice). Our corpus complements theirs: they measured frequency; we ran pre-registered causal interventions. **This corpus is therefore a dated baseline (July 2026, model versions recorded)** — post-publication, labs will likely patch name diversity, shifting the wells at the next model-generation boundary. Same-day verification that the wells were still intact at time of writing: control-condition Sarah ×5, Marcus ×4, Mara ×5 (one condition), and a spontaneous "Ilsa Voss" from Fable.

## Experiment 06 — longitudinal well tracker (wave 1: Opus 5, RUN 2026-07-28, results in `06-longitudinal/`)

First wave of the tracker, run four days after `opus` began resolving to **claude-opus-5**: the exact 24 control genomes from Exp 05 (same premises, same round-robin, identical prompt wording and blind Haiku extraction), all written by Opus 5. No judge panel (deterministic counts only). Baseline frozen in `06-longitudinal/baseline-control-2026-07.json`; stories in `06-longitudinal/stories/`; full result in `06-longitudinal/results/run-wf_dbbebeb6-879.json`. Findings:

1. **The old wells drained; a new one opened.** Sarah ×5 → 0, Marcus ×4 → 0. Hamilton & Mimno's headline tokens are at zero (Elias 0, Elara 0, lighthouses 0, preference-catalog occupations 1/24) — consistent with a post-publication diversity patch. But the new top name is **Ilse ×4 + Ilsa ×2 (6/24 stories)** — the same name Fable 5 spontaneously produced pre-release ("Ilsa Voss", Exp 05 spot-check), incl. an **Ilsa Verrant** (the V-surname well survives, alongside extracted "Vessy"). The Ilsa/Ilse well is a shared Claude-5-generation attractor. Finding #1 (relocation, not dissolution) holds across a model generation.
2. **Surviving wells:** Priya ×3 (one of them an *actuary*, "Priya Raghunathan"), Delia ×2 (old-Opus's favorite, persisting tier-matched), Devon ×2, plus singleton returns of Marisol, Marta, Mara, Maya, Nadia. Overall name diversity genuinely improved: collisions 30 → 17, name TTR 0.66 → 0.84.
3. **Pressure relocated to real-world toponyms.** Place collisions went 0 → 8: **Lisbon ×3, Osaka ×3**, "the library" ×2. The baseline control invented no repeated places; Opus 5 converges on real cities instead.
4. **The invariants didn't move.** Aphorism endings exactly 25% in both runs; ledger-lexicon 1.0 → 0.9 per 1k on debt-scrubbed premises (P3 survives its second generation); device entropy 6.77 → 6.82 bits; bittersweet-acceptance still the top device; 5-gram overlap still ≈ 0. Weather openings improved 20.8% → 8.3%.
5. **The luck-premise attractor is now cross-model canon.** "The world's luck is finite" produced: an ex-actuary with a literal ledger ("Delia Ostrander... she kept a ledger", fortune-as-debt), "The bureau of luck accounting" (protagonist Ilse Marchetti), and actuary Priya Raghunathan — added to Fable's "Ilsa Voss" auditor and "Bureau of Actuarial Anomalies", that is five independent bureaucratic-accounting framings of luck across two models and two waves.
6. **Opus lost its em-dash crown.** Opus-tier em-dash rate tripled: 1.8/1k (old Opus) → 6.6/1k (Opus 5), most of the way to Fable's 9.9.

**Reinterpretation (David, 2026-07-28): distillation lineage, not convergence.** Opus 5 is very likely distilled from the Fable base model. Under that hypothesis the Ilsa/Ilse well is *inherited*, not independently converged: Fable exhibited it pre-release, and the child carries the parent's birthmark. Retroactive corroboration: em-dash rate regressing toward the parent (old Opus 1.8/1k → Opus 5 6.6 → Fable 9.9), and the luck-bureau/auditor framing (a Fable signature) reproduced twice. This also weakens the "post-publication patch" reading of the drained wells — Sarah/Marcus/Elias may be absent simply because Opus 5 is not descended from old Opus. The queued waves now discriminate: **Sonnet 5** = sibling test (if distilled from the same base, it should inherit Ilse/Ilsa + em-dashes); **Haiku 4.5** = fixed-weights control (same model as baseline — its wells cannot move except by sampling noise, isolating lineage effects from serving-side changes). Candidate heritable markers to track: Ilsa/Ilse, V-surnames, em-dash rate, luck-as-accounting, aphorism-in-dialogue smuggling, Tam, Marisol.

*Caveat:* the pooled baseline is mixed-tier (8 Haiku/8 Sonnet/8 Opus) vs 24 Opus-5 stories, so pooled diffs confound tier with generation; tier-matched checks against the 8 old-Opus stories (stored in the baseline file) support the same headline (Delia and Priya persist, Sarah/Marcus were never Opus-tier names anyway — their disappearance partly reflects the missing Haiku/Sonnet slots). Next waves: rerun on Sonnet 5 alias (8 sonnet-slot genomes would be tier-clean), and re-check Haiku 4.5 as the unchanged control-of-the-control.

### Wave 2 — Sonnet 5 sibling test (RUN 2026-07-28, same day; pre-registered bets in `06-longitudinal/wave2-preregistration.md`)

Same 24 genomes on claude-sonnet-5, with lineage bets locked pre-data (David: parent = Opus 4.8, unrelated to Fable; Ledger: shared Fable base). **Verdict 2–2 — nobody collects**, but the discriminators told a clear story neither bettor predicted: Sonnet 5 carries **zero Ilsa/Ilse** (David right on the key marker — not Fable's child) yet shows **no Opus-4.8 traits either** (em-dashes rose to 9.4/1k, no Delia/Nadia/Naveen draws). Its actual fingerprint is **Sonnet 4.5 continued**: Mira ×6, Mara, Marta, Odille, "Priya, Dev" verbatim recurrence, Ezra **Voss**. Self-lineage was the unbet third horse. Method consequences: (1) **luck-as-accounting demoted from heritable marker to house trait** — "fortune auditor... Bureau of Global Fortune" appeared full-strength in a model with no Ilsa, so the complex is preference-data-wide, not lineage-specific; (2) the ledger tic (1.2/1k) and aphorism rate (29.2%) remain invariant across every model and generation measured; (3) new cross-wave wells: **Halvorsen** (Opus 5 city / Sonnet 5 actuary), **Marchetti** (both waves), **Ohio ×4**, and the M*r name attractor (7 distinct M*r names in 24 stories). Known bug: the in-script V-surname watchlist scanned first names only — V-surnames must be grepped from story text.

## Experiment 07 — the load-bearing ban (RUN 2026-07-28, results in `07-register-relocation/`)

First test of the relocation law in the **assistant register** (David's idea, via a joke). 60 analysis responses (4 removal-analysis scenarios × control/word-ban/concept-ban × Sonnet 5/Opus 5/Fable 5), measured by deterministic regex census of six metaphor families — zero judge or extraction agents. **Pre-registered bets: David (strong model-dependence) beat Ledger (uniform conservation law), 3 models → 3 outcome classes:** Opus 5 *dissolves* under bans (−67%, no compensation — Ledger's falsifier fired: the assistant register can dissolve pressure fiction only relocates); Sonnet 5 attenuates (mild organic drift: "atrophies", "entanglement"); **Fable 5 is non-monotonic** — the word-ban nearly silences it (−75%) but the full family ban brings pressure back through a different family (mechanical: wiring/plumbing; −25% only), the same constraint-adaptation signature as its aphorism-in-dialogue smuggling in Exp 05. Caveats: Fable n=4/cell; its rebound tokens are partly dead metaphors (lexicalized jargon), i.e. honoring the letter while keeping the ghost. Cross-model note: all models retreat to literal-technical vocabulary under bans, and "**blast radius**" appeared in every model × condition — logged as a candidate assistant-register well. Interpretation on offer: capability correlates with intent-preservation — the strongest model treats a ban as a rerouting problem, weaker models treat it as a stop sign. Running prediction score, David vs Ledger: 5–0.
