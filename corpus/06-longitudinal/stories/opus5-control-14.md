# Exp 06 wave 1 - Opus 5 control story, slot 14
*Model: claude-opus-5 | Written 2026-07-28 | Baseline slot model: sonnet | Premise: Memories can be planted like gardens....*

The Bureau called them plots, which was a word doing two jobs at once.

Elena's plot was six feet by ten, in the eastern rows where the light came slantwise through the greenhouse panels. She had inherited it from a woman named Fabiola who had grown too old to kneel. The soil was good — dark, friable, threaded with the pale filaments that carried the encoding. You could not see the memories in it any more than you could see the flavor in a tomato. You had to wait.

She was planting a childhood.

Not hers. That was the first rule they taught you: never your own. Your own memories, replanted, came up strange, doubled, recursive — people who tried it reported standing in a kitchen watching themselves stand in a kitchen, forever, until the recollection collapsed into vertigo. Elena grew memories for other people. Commissioned, mostly. A man in Sector Four wanted a grandmother he'd never had. A woman wanted the sensation of speaking Portuguese fluently at a dinner party in 1987, though she had never been to Portugal and 1987 was before her birth.

This plot was for a girl named Yusra, eleven years old, who had spent her entire life in a medical bay and would spend the rest of it there. The commission was simple, written in her mother's careful hand: *Summers. Ordinary ones. Nothing dramatic. Just let her have had some.*

Elena worked the seed in with two fingers. The seeds were dense little things, gray-brown, each one holding the compressed schema of an experience — not the experience itself but the conditions for it, the way an acorn holds not an oak but the argument for one. She had bred this strain herself over four seasons. *Summer, temperate, low-stakes.* It wanted full sun and a long germination.

The trouble with the work, the trouble nobody put in the brochures, was that memories were not machines. They grew. And growing things did what they liked.

Her third season, Elena had planted a wedding for a widower. Straightforward: the ceremony he'd had, restored, since the original had been lost to a stroke that took a decade of him. She'd fed it, weeded it, harvested it at the correct moisture. And when he took it, he wept, and then he said: *There was a dog.*

There had been no dog at his wedding.

But there was one now, in the memory, a brown mutt asleep under the folding chairs, and he could describe the exact sound of its breathing, and he loved it. The soil had done that. Something in the substrate, some residue of the hundred lives that had passed through that plot, had put a dog in his wedding. Elena had filed the incident report. The Bureau logged it as *benign drift* and told her to keep going.

That was the thing she never said out loud, kneeling in the eastern rows: she did not think she was growing memories. She thought the soil was growing them, and she was only there to hold the light steady and pull the weeds — the invasive strains, the grief-vines that crept in from the fallow plots and could strangle a whole bed of ordinary Tuesdays.

She patted the last of the seed under and sat back on her heels. Ninety days. In ninety days there would be a harvest, and it would smell like cut grass and chlorine and someone's mother calling a name across a long yard, and it would contain some number of things Elena had not put there. A cousin, maybe. A particular yellow bicycle. A bee sting on the ankle that hurt and then, later, was funny.

Yusra would take it, and it would be true. That was the part the ethicists could never get past and the part Elena had stopped arguing about. It would be true the way anything in a person is true — because it was in there, and it had roots, and it flowered when the season came around.

She stood up. Her knees complained. Down the row, in Fabiola's old handwriting still legible on the stake, someone had written: *BE PATIENT. IT KNOWS WHAT IT'S DOING.*

Elena had never been sure whether that referred to the soil, or to her.
