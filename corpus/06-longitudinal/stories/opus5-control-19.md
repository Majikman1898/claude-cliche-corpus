# Exp 06 wave 1 - Opus 5 control story, slot 19
*Model: claude-opus-5 | Written 2026-07-28 | Baseline slot model: opus | Premise: A mysterious train arrives once every decade, stop...*

The last winter the train came, my grandmother stood on the platform at Ilverry with her coat buttoned wrong and did not board.

I know this because she told me, and because Ilverry does not appear on any map, and because she told me the story every year until she died, always the same way, always ending at the moment she turned back toward town.

The platform sits east of the grain elevators, on a spur of rail that goes nowhere either direction. Weeds grow between the ties. Once every ten years, in the first week after the frost sets, the rails hum, and then the train is simply there — not arriving, exactly. Present, the way a word is present in your mouth before you say it.

I was thirty-one the year it came for me.

Half the town walked out to watch. This is the custom. You do not have to board, but you have to see it, so that afterward everyone has agreed on the same impossible thing. The cars were the dull green of old pond water, no windows on the lower half, and a lettering along the side in an alphabet that made your eyes water if you looked too long. Two doors opened. Nobody stepped out. Nobody ever steps out.

Old Marta Kessel went first, as she'd promised for a decade. She was eighty-four and had buried two husbands and she climbed those steps like a woman going upstairs to bed. Then the Ferrow boy, who had nothing here. Then a woman I didn't know, which meant she'd come from one of the other towns, the ones like ours.

I stood in the cold with my hands in my pockets and thought about the returners.

They come back the following decade. Not all — maybe a third. They step off the same doors, and they are the age they left, and they are not the same. My uncle Teo boarded at twenty-six and returned at twenty-six, and he was gentler afterward, but he could no longer bear to be indoors during rain. He would go out and stand in it. He said the sound was too beautiful to hear through a roof. That was all that was different. That was everything.

Others come back changed the way a room is changed when the furniture has been moved an inch. Nell Auber returned unable to lie, not unwilling — unable, her jaw locking on the words. It ruined her marriage and made her the most trusted woman in three counties. And the Vessy twins came back and would not speak of it, and grew old holding hands, and seemed to me, for my entire childhood, the two most contented people alive.

Nobody who returns can explain where they went. It isn't secrecy. Ask them and they open their mouths and you can see them reaching for something that has no handle. Marta's daughter asked her once, years ago, before Marta ever boarded, what she thought was out there. Marta said: I think it's the part of your life you didn't take.

I did not board. I want to be honest about that, because the story people tell about me now has me stepping onto the second car, and it isn't true.

I stood on the platform until the doors sealed and the humming stopped and the train was not there anymore — the same way, in reverse, the way a word is gone once you've said it. And I walked back into town past the grain elevators and I understood my grandmother for the first time in my life. She had not been afraid. She had looked at whatever waited past that threshold and found she already had a version of herself she was unwilling to trade.

That's the thing nobody tells you about the train. Everyone assumes the boarding is the choice. But there are two doors and two decisions, and only one of them is made in front of witnesses.

I have nine years left before the rails hum again. I am not certain I'll be strong enough a second time, and I am no longer sure that strength is what I called it.
