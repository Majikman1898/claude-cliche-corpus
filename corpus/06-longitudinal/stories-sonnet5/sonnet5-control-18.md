# Exp 06 wave 2 - Sonnet 5 control story, slot 18
*Model: claude-sonnet-5 | Written 2026-07-28 | Premise: People can swap memories like possessions...*

The trade was supposed to be temporary. That's what the broker had said, sliding the drive across the counter like it was nothing more than a loaner appliance. "Two weeks, then it comes back. You'll barely notice the gap."

Mira noticed the gap.

It sat where her mother's voice used to be — the specific timbre of it, saying her name across a kitchen in 1997. She'd traded that fragment, plus a summer at the lake house and the smell of her college dorm, for three years of someone else's confidence. A stranger's certainty, distilled and portable, currently doing something to the muscles around Mira's eyes that made her look, in the mirror, like a woman who had never doubted herself in her life.

It was working. That was the terrible part. She'd walked into the promotion meeting and not flinched once, not rehearsed her sentences four times before saying them, not apologized for taking up air. The confidence fit like a coat that had been tailored for someone slightly larger than her, roomy in places she hadn't known she was small.

But at night the gap ached. Not like a wound — more like the pressure change before weather, a barometric absence where her mother's voice should have filled the room. She found herself standing in the kitchen at 2 a.m., waiting for a call that used to come from nowhere, from inside her, and now simply didn't.

She went back to the exchange on a Tuesday. It was in a strip mall between a nail salon and a place that sold phone cases, fluorescent-lit, smelling of ozone and coffee left too long on a burner.

"You want to buy it back," the broker said. Not a question. He had the tired patience of a man who'd seen this a hundred times, people returning for the pieces of themselves they'd sold too cheap.

"I want my mother's voice."

He tapped at his terminal. "Depends who's holding it now. Some people integrate fast — memory takes root, becomes theirs, and then it's not really a clean transfer anymore. You'd be buying a copy of a copy. Fainter."

"How much fainter?"

"Won't know till you have it." He shrugged, not unkindly. "That's the thing nobody tells you going in. Memories aren't like furniture. Furniture doesn't wear grooves into the person using it. You sell a couch, you get the same couch back. You sell a Tuesday afternoon with your mother, somebody else lives inside it for two weeks, and it's not the same shape anymore. It's got their fingerprints on it now too."

Mira thought about the confidence, how it already didn't feel like a coat anymore — how she'd stopped noticing the seams. In another few weeks it would just be her walk, her voice in meetings, indistinguishable from the woman she used to be. Was that a violation or was that simply how a person grew? She didn't know anymore which parts of herself had always been there and which had been bought at a counter like this one, secondhand, still warm from somebody else's living.

"Do it," she said. "Buy it back. Whatever's left of it."

The broker's fingers moved. Somewhere in a server farm outside the city, in a building that hummed with other people's Christmases and first kisses and the particular grief of specific goodbyes, a record adjusted. A voice — thinner now, worn at the edges by a stranger's use — began its slow trip home.

It arrived that night like a word almost remembered, her mother's voice saying her name, fainter than she recalled but unmistakably hers, unmistakably it, filling the gap the way water fills a container: not the same as before, not whole, but present, and asking nothing of her except that she keep it, this time, and let it wear its own groove instead of somebody else's.
