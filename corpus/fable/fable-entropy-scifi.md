The anomaly took the barge *Meridian Cross* the way frost takes a window: slowly, then all at once. One shift Johan was ladling stew for six crew; the next, the reactor was dead, the water recycler was dead, and the stars off the port side had begun to bend like spoons in hot tea.

Johan was forty-eight, a barge cook of the exacting kind, and he had one small gift: a palate calibrated finer than any sensor. He could taste a failing filter two weeks before the diagnostics flagged it. He tasted the anomaly before anyone saw it — a copper wrongness in the morning coffee, the same wrongness Captain Iris had described in her logs eleven years ago, before the inquiry board laughed her out of her license and she died with *delusional* stamped in her file.

Iris had trained him. Iris had believed him about the coffee. Nobody had believed Iris.

They drifted nine days. On the tenth, a salvage cutter matched their tumble, and a woman named Cassandra crossed on a tether with power cells strapped to her back like wings.

"Six lives," she said afterward, in the galley Johan had restored to order. "Fuel, cells, a burned tow rig. You understand what that costs me." She smiled when she said it, and she praised his soup, and she praised him — *a man with your palate, wasted on a barge* — and Johan, who kept every rule and paid every debt, felt the obligation settle over him like a yoke. There was no number attached. That was the trap of it. A debt without a number can never be paid down.

The barge crew dispersed at the next station. Johan stayed. It was the rule he lived by: you square your accounts. For fourteen months he cooked aboard Cassandra's cutter, and for fourteen months she fed him flattery with the patience of a woman salting a field. *Only you tasted it coming, Johan. Instruments lie; you don't. Iris would have wanted a witness like you.* By the eighth month he had told her everything — Iris's logs, the coordinates, the copper taste. By the twelfth he believed the expedition was his own idea.

They went back in the fourteenth month. Johan stood at the sensor board tasting cup after cup of recycled water, calling the gradient like a sounding line — *sharper, sharper, dead metal now, hold* — guiding the cutter along the anomaly's rim where instruments showed only clean vacuum. The cameras caught what Iris had sworn to: a region where light folded and time ran grainy, real and mapped and undeniable. He wept at his station. The board would have to reopen her file. The word *delusional* would come off.

Then Cassandra encrypted the data under her salvage claim.

"Discovery rights," she said gently, kindly, the way she said everything. "You were crew. Crew labor belongs to the boat — you know the rules, Johan. You love the rules." And he did, that was the poison of it; he had checked every regulation except the one that mattered, because she had spent fourteen months telling him he was too fine an instrument to be cheated.

He got one thing. Buried in the claim's mandatory registry entry, one line of provenance she couldn't legally strike: *initial detection per method of Capt. Iris (deceased), corroborated.* Twelve words in a public record. Iris, proved right at last, in a footnote to another woman's fortune.

Johan took his back pay and a berth on an outbound barge. The debt was declared settled — Cassandra could afford generosity now. In the new galley he brewed the first pot of coffee and tasted it, out of habit, out of pride, and found it perfectly, blandly clean.

He had won. Somewhere in a registry, Iris was vindicated forever. He poured the cup out anyway, and did not trust the next kind word he heard, ever again.
