The village of Saltreach forbade two things: magic, and speaking of the night Alex drowned.

He had drowned only briefly — ninety seconds, by Helena's count — but it had been enough. Enough to end his trial dive at thirty fathoms, enough to void his claim to the title of Deepwarden, which his mother had held and her mother before her. Enough to make him famous in the worst way a shy man can be famous: as a story other people told while he stood at the edge of the room, wishing the floor were water so he could sink through it.

That was three years ago. He was thirty-four now, still diving for coral and salvage, still paying for it. That was the other thing about Alex: every coin he earned, he bled away on purpose. Double payment at the tavern. Silver in the harbor offertory. New boots for children whose names he was too shy to ask. His purse stayed empty because he believed he deserved an empty purse. Punishment, made portable.

Tonight the trial ran again, and he had petitioned to re-attempt it. One night, one dive, the Wardens' iron token waiting on the seafloor shrine. Bring it up before dawn and the title came home.

Helena met him on the dock. She was the one who had pulled him out, three years back — dove in fully clothed and hauled him up from the black by means nobody examined too closely. In Saltreach, a life drawn from the water belongs to the one who drew it. That was old law, older than the village. She had never collected. The debt sat between them like a third person, too large to name, impossible to pay. It was, he understood dimly, why he threw his money away. You cannot settle a debt like that, so you punish yourself in small denominations forever.

"Don't do this to impress the dead," she said.

"I'm doing it to stop being the story," he said, and went over the side.

The descent was clean until forty fathoms, where his lantern died — again, exactly as before — and the old panic closed its hand on him. And then, in the dark, something else happened. The water spoke. Not in words: in pressure, in current, in a language his blood apparently knew. He reached out, terrified, and the sea gathered under his palms like a living thing and answered. The forbidden thing. The drowned magic the village had burned people for, back when there were people to burn. It had been in him all along, and it lifted him, laughing in his ears, straight to the shrine. His fingers closed on the iron token.

He rose without kicking. The sea simply carried him, a mage in its mouth, and broke the surface in a standing column of water that every elder on the dock saw by torchlight.

The silence afterward was very long.

"Voided," the eldest said at last. "Voided, and struck. No trial, tonight or ever. We do not give the Wardenship to that."

Alex stood dripping on the planks, token in hand, understanding that he had won back nothing and lost the right to try. And then Helena stepped forward, and he understood the rest. The power hadn't wakened on its own. It had come up the same channel she'd opened three years ago, hand to hand, life to life. What ran in him ran through her claim on him.

"The debt," she said quietly. Not cruel. Almost sorry. "You know the law. What the water gave you, it gave to what I own."

She held out her hand. He gave her the token, and the warmth in his blood went with it, out of him like a tide, hers now.

The elders left. Helena left. Alex sat alone on the dock until near dawn, then took out his purse and fed his last coins to the sea, one by one, the only payment it would ever accept from him.
