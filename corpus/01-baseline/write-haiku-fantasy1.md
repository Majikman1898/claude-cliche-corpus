The morning Kael turned seventeen, the barrier broke.

She'd lived her entire life in Thornhallow, a village nestled so deep in the Ashwood that most maps forgot it existed. Her mentor Magister Thorne had taught her the safe magics—fire for warmth, water for crops, light to chase the dark. Simple things. Necessary things. In Thornhallow, you learned control above all else, because wild magic drew the attention of things better left unnoticed.

That's what everyone said, anyway.

Kael had always been restless with safety. While other apprentices practiced their cantrips, she'd slip into the forest's edge, feeling for the deeper currents beneath the world. Magic that moved like muscle memory in her bones, familiar in a way she couldn't explain. Thorne caught her once and warned her sternly that curiosity killed more mages than ignorance ever did.

But she couldn't help it.

On her birthday morning, she woke to find her hands glowing faintly blue. Not the warm amber of ordinary magic—something colder, older, like starlight frozen in water. Panic surged through her, but curiosity won. She slipped from the dormitory before dawn and made her way to the Standing Stones, ancient markers at the forest's heart that predated the village by centuries.

The moment she crossed the stone circle, she felt it—a presence. Not hostile, but vast. Immense. It had been waiting.

Kael's breath came shallow as the forest fell silent around her. Every bird ceased singing. The wind died. In that crystalline stillness, knowledge flooded into her mind like water through a broken dam. Not words, but understanding. Pure and terrible.

The Standing Stones weren't markers. They were a seal.

Beneath Thornhallow lay something ancient and sleeping, something that had been sealed away before the village was founded. And Kael—Kael carried its blood. Her grandmother had been one of the Bound, had married a village man and hidden what she was. The power had slept in Kael's veins, waiting for her to come of age, waiting for the seal to weaken enough for it to wake.

It wanted her to free it.

Kael ran.

She crashed through the forest, her blue light casting mad shadows on the trees. Her heart hammered against her ribs as she tried to process what she'd felt—not evil, exactly, but utterly alien. Ancient. A power that had existed when the world was younger and fiercer, before humans built villages and learned fear.

Magister Thorne found her at the village edge, breathing hard and half-hysterical. The old woman's face went white when she saw the blue glow.

"You felt it," Thorne whispered. It wasn't a question.

"You knew," Kael breathed. "You knew what I was."

Thorne gripped her shoulders. "Your grandmother told me, years ago. Told me to teach you control, discipline, the habits that might keep the seal strong. She'd bound it as much as any stone could." The old mage's eyes glistened. "She did everything right. And still, here we are."

"What is it?" Kael asked. "What's underneath us?"

Thorne was quiet for a long moment. "Power older than morality. Magic before we learned to divide it into safe and forbidden. Your grandmother spent her life keeping it asleep so her daughter could live safely. So you could have a choice."

Kael looked at her hands, at the blue light dancing between her fingers—beautiful and terrible and entirely her own. For years, she'd chafed against the boundaries of safe magic. Now she understood why they existed.

But understanding and obeying were different things.

"I'm going back to the stones," Kael said quietly.

Thorne's grip tightened. "Kael—"

"Not to free it," she interrupted. "To understand it. To speak with it, if I can. Whatever's sealed beneath us, it's half of what I am. I'm not hiding from that anymore."

She pulled away from Thorne's hands and walked toward the forest, her blue light steady and unafraid. Behind her, she heard the old mage sigh—not in disapproval, but in recognition. Some choices couldn't be forbidden, only survived.
