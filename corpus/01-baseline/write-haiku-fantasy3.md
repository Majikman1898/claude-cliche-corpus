Kael had always been small for his age, a trait he shared with most of the apprentice mages in Thornhaven. The village clung to a rocky hillside where little grew except pine and heather, where magic was practical and muted—cantrips to warm hearth fires, charms to ease childbirth, nothing more. Spectacular displays were frowned upon. Dangerous.

His master, Old Merin, taught him the approved spells in the tower's highest room. Merin's hands trembled when he demonstrated even minor illusions, as if the magic itself frightened him. "Enough," he would say after only an hour of practice. "Rest now. The mind grows weary."

But Kael's mind never grew weary. At night, when Merin snored in the chambers below, Kael studied the banned books locked in the iron chest—the ones he'd learned to open months ago with a simple touch-spell Merin never taught him.

The books spoke of something called the Binding, a sealing placed on magic centuries ago by the Council of First Mages. They described how human magic had once burned bright enough to reshape stone and summon storms. Then the Binding came, limiting all practitioners to safe, small magics. The books were unclear about why, but they hinted at catastrophe. Failed experiments. Cities unmade.

One night, Kael found a text he'd missed before: a slim volume bound in leather that flaked at the edges. The author's name was smudged, but the contents made his breath catch. Instructions. Not for spells, but for something else. A way to feel beneath the Binding, to touch the power that had been hidden away.

The ritual required three things: moonless night, a place where old magic had pooled, and sacrifice.

He knew where to find the second. Beneath the village, where the stone grew warm even in winter, where no plants would grow and animals refused to tread. The old folk said the ground there was sick. Kael had always felt drawn to it instead.

He waited for the new moon and made his way down into the deep dark, a candle flickering in one trembling hand. At the ritual site, he spoke the words from the book. They felt strange in his mouth, wrong in a way that made his teeth ache. 

For the sacrifice, he'd brought a small bird, already dead, found by the path. His heart hammered as he completed the words. Nothing happened.

Then everything happened.

The Binding didn't crack—Kael realized it wasn't real stone or walls at all. It was more like a pressure, a weight pressing down on his mind that he'd never noticed because it had always been there. Like noticing you have a body only when it's removed.

Without it, magic blazed through him in colors that didn't have names. He felt the potential singing in his blood, the raw infinite possibility he'd been born with and never known. With barely a thought, the candle flame became a column of light that lit the entire cavern. The stone beneath his feet shimmered. He could see the bones of the earth, the slow rivers of magic flowing through everything, the web connecting all living things.

He could destroy Thornhaven with a thought. Rebuild it. Fly.

The terror was worse than the ecstasy.

Kael closed his eyes and pulled back, hand over hand, as if dragging himself up from drowning. The Binding slammed back into place like a door slamming shut. He gasped, on his knees, the magic screaming silently against its cage.

When he emerged hours later into false dawn, Old Merin was waiting at the tower door, face gray as ash.

"You've done it," Merin whispered. Not a question. "I felt it break. Just for a moment, I felt it."

He reached out as if to touch Kael's face, then withdrew, afraid.

"What have they made us?" Kael asked.

Merin had no answer.
