Kael had always been the weakest mage in Thornwick. While her peers conjured flames that danced across the stone training grounds, she could barely summon a spark. While they levitated boulders, she struggled with pebbles. Master Thorne had suggested three times that perhaps she should take up blacksmithing instead.

But Kael persisted, spending hours alone in the crypt beneath the village library, studying the forbidden texts that no proper mage was supposed to read. The books smelled of copper and ash, and their pages crumbled at the slightest touch. Most contained warnings written in angular script: *Do not attempt. Sealed by the First Council. Death awaits.*

One evening, as autumn rain hammered against the stone walls, Kael found a passage she'd somehow missed before. It described something called the Null—not an absence of magic, but its inverse. A power that didn't create or manipulate, but undid. Unmade. Erased.

The text was fragmented, half the pages missing, but one line repeated: *The Null consumes all that is woven. Only the desperate or the mad would dare touch it.*

Kael wondered if she qualified as either.

She returned to the training grounds the next morning, where Master Thorne was demonstrating fire manipulation to the newer students. His flames leaped between his fingers like obedient serpents. Kael watched, as always, feeling the familiar hollow ache of inadequacy.

"Come," Thorne said, noticing her. "Try again. Perhaps today will be different."

She gathered her concentration, drawing on the magic that lived somewhere deep in her chest. A pale wisp emerged from her palm, barely visible. The other students didn't bother hiding their smirks.

Then Kael did something she'd never consciously done before. Instead of trying to create, she reached inward and pulled the other way. She thought of nothing. Of emptiness. Of the space between heartbeats.

The wisp didn't grow brighter or stronger. It simply stopped being.

But that wasn't the remarkable part. Where it had disappeared, a perfect sphere of absolute stillness formed in the air. The rain froze mid-fall around it. The wind died. Master Thorne's flames guttered and went out—not extinguished, but unmade, as though they'd never existed at all.

The sphere lasted only a breath before collapsing. Reality rushed back in with a sound like breaking glass. Rain resumed its descent. Wind howled across the grounds.

Master Thorne stared at his empty hands, his expression unreadable. "That," he said slowly, "should not be possible."

He forbade her from returning to the grounds. Over the following weeks, he appeared at her home three times, each visit more agitated than the last. On his final visit, he told her what she'd suspected: the Null had been purged from the world four hundred years ago, sealed away by mages who'd recognized it as an affront to nature itself.

"It's not inherently evil," she tried to argue.

"No," Thorne said grimly. "But it is inherently dangerous. To you most of all. Powers like that consume their wielders. Every mage who learned the Null eventually used it on themselves, trying to erase the guilt. The fear. The loneliness. Until there was nothing left to erase."

Kael received her exile notice a month later. She was banished from Thornwick, forbidden to practice magic within the region's borders. As she walked the mountain pass for the last time, her small bag of possessions on her back, she felt the power stirring in her chest—curious, patient, hungry.

She thought of Thorne's warning. Of all those mages who'd erased themselves into nothing. Then she clenched her fists and kept walking down toward the world below, where no one yet knew what she could do.
