The *Meridian's Crossing* had been drifting through the Kessler Expanse for three weeks when the sensors started singing.

Captain Yara Okonkwo was halfway through her coffee when the first alert chimed. She set down her cup in the observation lounge, watching the starfield shimmer like heat haze. Not normal. Nothing about this was normal.

"Report," she said into her comm.

Kej Vance's voice crackled back from the engine room, his accent thickening with stress. "Captain, I'm seeing power fluctuations across all systems. Not critical yet, but it's like something's... tugging at us."

Yara walked to the main viewport. The void stretched endlessly, black and indifferent. She'd been commanding this salvage vessel for five years, crossing the empty spaces between inhabited worlds, searching for derelict ships worth salvaging. She'd seen solar flares and radiation storms, asteroid fields and gravitational anomalies. She'd never seen the stars themselves seem to *pulse*.

"I'm heading to the bridge," she said.

The bridge crew was already scrambling. Lysander, her navigator, had his hands dancing across the helm controls, trying to compensate for something he clearly couldn't quantify. Their chief scientist, Dr. Amara Koss, was hunched over her analysis station, her dark eyes reflecting cascading data streams.

"Captain." Amara looked up. "There's a spatial distortion approximately two hundred kilometers off our port bow. It's not on any star charts. It's not... it shouldn't exist."

"Elaborate," Yara said, moving to the center of the bridge.

"It's a region of perfectly normal space," Amara said slowly, as if working through the contradiction even as she spoke. "But the radiation signature is wrong. The quantum fluctuations are wrong. It's like someone took a section of space and changed its fundamental rules. And Captain—it's moving. Toward us."

The bridge fell silent except for the white noise of cycling systems and Lysander's breathing.

"Speed?" Yara asked.

"Forty kilometers per hour, closing."

Yara made a decision. "Lysander, get us moving. Any direction away from it. Vance, I need full power to the engines. Amara, I want every sensor we have focused on that thing. I want to know what it is, where it came from, and why it's following us."

The *Meridian's Crossing* lurched forward, its old engines groaning. Yara gripped the armrest of her captain's chair. They were fast enough for salvage work, but not fast enough for combat or true escape. They were merchant hunters, not warships. The crew was seven people.

"It's not following," Amara said after a moment. "Its trajectory is constant. We're moving away from it, but its vector doesn't change based on our movement. It's moving through space on its own path."

Relief and unease warred in Yara's chest. "What happens if we run into it?"

"That's the problem," Amara whispered. "I don't know. The laws of physics as I understand them break down at the boundary. It's like—" She paused. "It's like trying to describe color to someone who's only ever seen in black and white. I don't have the vocabulary."

Yara watched the stars. They were still pulsing, she realized. Not the stars themselves, but the light reaching them had changed. It was as if reality itself was taking shallow breaths.

"Log it," she told Lysander. "Everything. Coordinates, sensor data, our course and speed. Whatever that thing is, nobody else has documented it. Not in any database we have access to."

"Captain," Kej's voice returned through the comm. "Engines are maxed out. We're clear of the distortion's current path. But I'm reading something else. There are more of them. At least three others, all throughout this sector."

Yara felt her stomach tighten. Outside the viewport, the black void seemed to deepen, to hold depths it hadn't held before. She thought of the old maritime explorers of Earth, sailing into unmapped oceans, never knowing what lay beyond the horizon.

"Hold course," she said finally. "And somebody get me more coffee."
