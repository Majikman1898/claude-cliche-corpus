The *Cormorant* had been running dark for six days straight when the sensors picked up the signal.

"Captain, you should see this," Maya said from her station, her voice tight in a way that made the back of Captain Okafor's neck prickle. On a research vessel this small, tension traveled fast.

Okafor leaned over the readings. The anomaly occupied a sphere roughly three kilometers in diameter, floating in the absolute nothing between the Kersel Gate and Waystation Nine. There was no stellar body nearby, no debris field that might explain it. Just empty space, and then this.

"It's not reading as matter," Maya continued. "Our instruments say it exists, but they can't measure it. It's like... empty in a way that shouldn't be possible."

The rest of the crew filtered in—Ezra from engineering, Dr. Shen from the lab, and young Marcus at comms. Five people to crew the *Cormorant*, which was just enough when everything went right.

"Could be a gravitational lensing effect," Shen suggested, squinting at the data. "Something massive and dark, bending light around itself."

"Or a sensor ghost," Ezra offered. "We've been running the scanners hot. Maybe something fried."

Okafor knew better than to dismiss any theory, but her instincts said this was real. And real unknowns in deep space were rarely friendly. "Keep us at five thousand kilometers. Full passive scan. I want to know everything: radiation, gravitational fluctuations, exotic particles."

For the next hour, they measured. The anomaly didn't move. It didn't emit anything. Yet every instrument they pointed at it simply failed to return what should be there.

Then Marcus looked up from comms. "Captain, I'm getting something. Audio."

"From where?" Okafor moved to his station.

"Inside the anomaly. It's faint, but it's definitely there."

The crew held their breath as he amplified the signal. What came through was a sound Okafor had never heard before—not words, not music, but something in between. It cycled through frequencies like a whale song played on an instrument that didn't exist, haunting and complex.

"It's mechanical," Ezra said. "Has to be. The pattern's too regular."

But Shen was shaking her head. "Look at the harmonic structure. It's not mechanical. It's alive. Or it was."

Okafor felt the weight of command settle heavier on her shoulders. She'd joined the exploration service specifically for moments like this—the real discoveries, not cargo runs and supply stops. But discovery meant risk.

"Take us closer," she said. "One kilometer approach. Slow."

The *Cormorant* crept forward. As they drew nearer, the signal became clearer, and Marcus turned pale. "Captain, I think... I think I'm picking up variations in the pattern. Like it's responding to us."

The anomaly still appeared as nothing—a sphere of empty reading on their screens. But the closer they came, the more the song changed, spiraling through new frequencies. It wasn't threatening. It sounded almost hopeful.

"It's a beacon," Shen whispered. "My God, it's been waiting."

Okafor's hand hovered over the navigation controls. She could order an immediate retreat. File a report. Let someone else with a bigger ship and a larger crew come investigate in six months or a year. It was the safe choice.

She thought of all the discoveries humanity had made by choosing not to turn away. The gates. The habitable worlds. The other civilizations that had sent their own small ships into the dark, looking for connection.

"Marcus, try to answer," she said. "Mirror the signal pattern back to it."

As his fingers moved across the keyboard, the song changed again, rising in pitch, building toward something neither mechanical nor musical. The *Cormorant* hummed with the frequency, and somewhere in the dark between stars, something ancient and patient reached out to touch them.
